Questions buyers actually ask

What you're probably wondering. Plain answers.

I get it's important. But why not wait until after launch?+
You answered this yourself on the call: "The only way to do it properly is to build towards it, not just launch it in one day and hope everyone understands what we're talking about." The audience that buys the agentic product on launch day has to already know what Walnut stands for, and that takes 8-10 weeks of compounding before launch. Starting after launch means the announcement lands in a cold audience and the next quarter goes to backfilling what could have been built upstream. Section 03 has the math on what each week of delay costs you specifically.
Why not just have ChatGPT or Claude do this?+
You can. Most LinkedIn content already does. Originality.ai's 2025 study of 3,368 posts from 99 influential profiles flagged 53.7% of long-form LinkedIn content as likely AI-generated. That's exactly the problem. The reason posts hitting your CRO's feed feel forgettable is they all read the same: generic operator-life takes written from the same prompts. We use AI heavily for the unsexy work (scraping what Levie and Robinson post, tracking the AI-GTM bench, watching how your competitors actually show up in feed, keeping your context graph fresh as the category moves), and real humans on our team write every post. The system gives us category fluency on day one; the humans give Oren something the algorithm hasn't already seen 10,000 times this week.
Our buyers are CROs and enterprise sellers. Are they really on LinkedIn?+
Yes, and more importantly, that's where they spend their consideration time. They're reading peers and watching how the category narrative forms. CROs don't read marketing emails; they read the LinkedIn timeline. The voices Oren competes with for mindshare (Robinson, Levie, the AI-GTM bench) all post there for the same reason.
What if Oren doesn't want to post, or hates the drafts?+
Nothing ships without Oren's approval. The five-minute Slack review is a feature, not a workaround. If a draft isn't right, we rewrite. If the system isn't clicking by day 30, we pause and re-scope, no clawback fights. The 90-day window is structured so the call gets made on real data, not vibes.
Won't I run out of angles after a few months? The 300-post problem.+
Your words on the call: "10, 20, 30 topics, sure. But 300?" Most founders posting weekly run out of fresh angles by month six. Our Context Graph is the structural answer: it constantly resurfaces new trends, competitor movements, adjacent conversations, plus whatever the intake forms from you and the team push in on a rolling basis. The bigger answer is that your POV (the third option of buyer enablement, with operators becoming AI champions instead of replacements) has years of depth in it. We don't need 300 hot takes. We need 3-4 strong positions, restated and evolved against whatever's happening in GTM that week.
What happens after 90 days?+
If the launch lands and the audience is real, we keep going (month-to-month, same rate, still no auto-renewal). If it isn't working, we shake hands and you keep every post, the voice node, the context graph, and the playbook for running it in-house. The 90 days are designed so the renew/walk call gets made on real data, not vibes.

Walnut owns everything. Every post, the full Context Graph, the voice node, the 90-day cadence playbook. Walk after the pilot and you walk with all of it. We don't gate it behind continued retainer.
